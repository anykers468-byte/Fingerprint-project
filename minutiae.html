Minutiae: the fine details

Fingerprint patterns group prints. Minutiae provide the smaller details that help distinguish one fingerprint from another.

1. Ridge ending

A ridge ending occurs when a friction ridge terminates within the fingerprint instead of continuing further.

Fingerprint systems can record the location and direction of this ridge ending.

2. Bifurcation

A bifurcation occurs when one friction ridge divides into two separate ridges that continue beyond the split.

The point where the ridge divides is an important location for fingerprint comparison.

3. Dot

A dot is a very short, isolated ridge fragment. It is much shorter than an ordinary ridge.

Its identification depends on the quality and detail of the fingerprint image.

4. Enclosure

An enclosure occurs when a ridge splits into two branches and then rejoins, creating a small enclosed space.

The enclosed shape is sometimes described as a lake or eye-shaped feature.

Why do minutiae matter?

Fingerprint recognition systems can use the positions and directions of minutiae to compare fingerprint samples. The quality of the fingerprint image affects how accurately these details can be detected.
